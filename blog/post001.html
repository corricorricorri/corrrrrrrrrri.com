01. HOW TO EXIST ONLINE WITHOUT TRYING TOO HARD Finding dignity in your own rhythm. Sprinkling intention into the small gestures, noticing the quiet weight of others, and other meditations on staying unbothered in a performative world. - - - - - - - - - - - - -

Monday was cold here in Texas. Not “light jacket” cold, but the kind that gives you permission to actually dress. And if you know anything about this state, you know those days are rare, and therefore sacred. Truly an excuse to layer the fuck up.

I pulled out an M65 I’d been holding onto, waiting for the perfect time to pair it with something snazzy—like this worn-in red Ralph button-down I’d thrifted just the day before. The shirt had clearly been through the wash a few too many times. Wayyyyyy too many, if we’re being honest. But all that laundering gave it a perfect, worn-in fade.

Lately I’ve been leaning into amekaji and ivy, which felt like enough justification to finally break in a pair of full-grain leather Allen Edmonds I’d scored for basically nothing at the Goodwill. Altogether, it was one of those rare fits where everything just worked. Naturally, I took a few flicks. Queued 'em up for Instagram and then… hesitated.

The jacket’s good, the shirt’s faded just right—fits like this are too rare not to snap.

For a brief moment I was paralyzed by the idea of performance…or being a "performative male." The phrase sounds ridiculous, but it tapped into something I couldn’t quite articulate. Still, the fit was good, so the pictures went up. Afterwards, I did something I almost never do: I got personal online, asking myself the same question I’d just asked: “As a man, how do you post fits to the ’gram without being performative?”

The anxiety isn’t new. Fear of being misunderstood is basically the cost of existing in public. Perception, if we define it, is the endless network of alleys, side streets, and blind corners where other people decide who you are. To be perceived is to be known, at least a little. To be seen. But what happens when people don’t like what they see? Not because you lack taste—you’ve got taste—but because you’re too cool.

I can feel the confusion from here. You’re thinking: how could I not be universally adored online? My vibes are immaculate. My fits are already doing stupid numbers in r/MFA. So what’s the problem?

Hate to break it to you, brother, but the days of posting a clean #OOTD without someone diagnosing you as a peacocking fuckboy are mostly behind us. A man can’t simply step outside in a good jacket anymore. Not without a little suspicion. Still, if you happen to belong to the ever-shrinking demographic of “chill dudes,” there may yet be a way through this. Consider this your field guide for living a non-performative life online.
